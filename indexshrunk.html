          Help

          Before you plant anything

          Three things worth doing in this order — skipping them is the most common way people end up frustrated with this app.

          1. Add this to your Home Screen — right now, before anything else

          Using it from a plain browser tab can show old, out-of-date information as you work, which gets confusing fast. Takes 10 seconds: iPhone — tap Share, then scroll down and tap "Add to Home Screen." If you don't see it in the list, tap "Edit Actions" at the bottom first and add it from there. Android — tap the ⋮ menu, then "Add to Home screen."

          2. Lay out your beds and structures before planting anything

          Get the physical layout right first — everything else builds on it, and a large plot genuinely takes a while to set out properly.

          3. Back up your bare plot once it's laid out, before adding any crops

          Setup → "Keep a backup." Doing this once, before planting, gives you a clean copy to always get back to. Keep that first file to one side from your regular, ongoing backups.

          What each tab does

          My Plot

          Your plot drawn to scale — beds, greenhouse, shed, containers, all in their real positions and sizes. Tap anything to see or edit it. "Coming up" below the map shows what's due soonest.

          Tasks

          A straightforward to-do list. Add anything by hand, with a date and how often it repeats. The app also adds its own tasks — buying seed, preparing a bed, sowing — based on what's been planned, so this fills up on its own too.

          Crops

          Everything currently growing, all in one place, whichever bed or tray it's actually in.

          Wishlist

          Somewhere to jot an idea down before it's forgotten — a crop, a tool to buy, something a fellow gardener grows. Type a crop name the app recognises and it sets up proper sowing reminders on its own; anything else is just saved as a note.

          History

          Everything harvested or cleared, as far back as it's been recorded — including vague memories from years ago, even without an exact bed or date. Searchable by date, name, bed, or crop.

          Planning ahead

          For anything not planted yet. Move an idea over from the Wishlist, or add one directly, then check it against every bed to see what would actually suit it — including genuine companion pairings and any disease or rotation warnings specific to that bed's own past.

          Weather

          The coming week at a glance, plus alerts for frost, storms, or heavy rain if those are turned on below.

          Notes

          Anything that doesn't belong anywhere else — practical reminders like which fuel canister is two-stroke. Keep a note private, or share it with other gardeners on the same plot.

          Setup

          Everything about the plot itself and how the app behaves — its size and shape, your postcode for weather, sharing with other gardeners, and keeping a backup.

          Sharing with another gardener

          Turn on sharing, then "Invite another gardener" gives you an invite code to send them. The reliable way to get them connected: they paste that code into "Received an invitation?" on their own Setup tab, from inside their own copy of My Plot.

          Tapping a shared link always opens a plain browser tab, never an app already sitting on someone's Home Screen — that's an iPhone/Android limitation, not something this app can work around. So if they've already added My Plot to their Home Screen, sending them a link means sharing only gets set up in that one-off browser tab, not in the actual app they'll use day to day — it can look like it worked, then seem to need doing all over again. Pasting the code directly into their installed app avoids that entirely.

          The numbers shown while you drag something

          Moving or resizing a bed on the plot, or a crop within a bed, shows a small readout with two numbers — how far it is from the left edge, and how far up from the bottom edge. This is true whether you're moving a whole bed around the plot or just a crop's box within one. It only really matters if a position ever looks odd after resizing something — the distance from the left and from the bottom stays fixed, so widening or heightening a bed or the plot itself can shift where things end up relative to the edges you're looking at.
